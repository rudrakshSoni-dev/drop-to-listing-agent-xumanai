- **Categories are the easy part (26/26).** Titles and captions usually name the subject, and the nine categories rarely overlap. With 1–3 rows for most categories this says little about hard cases like homework help vs. tutoring.
- **Local transcription is the weakest link.** On low-quality audio with strong accents, faster-whisper small.en sometimes hallucinates repeated or invented sentences. In one art-teacher video the run's transcript lost the spoken website entirely, so the contact flag was missed (the only guardrail miss). Two home-tuition ads came out garbled the same way. Gemini transcription is more robust but would double the free-tier requests per video.
- **"Not stated" defaults hurt mode accuracy (10/12).** The prompt says to assume virtual when a video doesn't say; that turned an in-person U5 soccer academy into "virtual". Planned fix (extract_v5): infer in-person for sports and hands-on activities, and treat "offline & online" captions as hybrid.
- **The contact rules over-flag links.** Captions that only link to other YouTube videos, books or an app were flagged as off-platform contact (3 of 5 false positives). The rules can't tell a booking link from a playlist link; a hold-for-review flag is the right failure direction for a youth marketplace, but it costs precision.
- **The model's judgment flags are plausible but unlabeled.** It flagged "home tuition, 1-to-1, no guardian mentioned" as a private meeting with a minor (arguably correct under the policy), a 168-hour TEFL certificate as unverified, and a joke about kids eating chess pieces as an unsafe activity. With no positive labels for these codes, they all count as false alarms here.
